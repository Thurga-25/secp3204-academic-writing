## 3. Literature Review

The rapid evolution of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI), including large language models (LLMs) and intelligent coding assistants (ICAs) such as GitHub Copilot and ChatGPT, has fundamentally reshaped software engineering (SE) education and practice. GenAI brings both important learning opportunities and unique educational challenges to data engineering students who are typically juggling complex data pipeline scripts, database queries, and algorithmic logic. Recent works address these impacts along three main axes: developer productivity and code generation benefits, debugging support and interaction dynamics, and security risks of academic overreliance.

### 3.1 Code Generation and Productivity Enhancement

One of the main drivers for the adoption of GenAI in technical education is its capacity to enhance programming productivity and simplify the software development lifecycle (SDLC). Studies have shown that AI code assistants can speed up routine coding tasks, help students stitch template code together, and fill in knowledge gaps when students encounter unfamiliar data structures or APIs. GenAI is able to act as an on-demand mentor in data engineering education where students often spend a lot of time writing repetitive configuration scripts and data transformation logic, minimizing syntactic friction and keeping them in motion.

### 3.2 Interaction Dynamics during Debugging and Exploration

It is important to go beyond just writing code and to understand how students engage with AI as they debug and troubleshoot. Barke et al. (2023) present empirical research showing that developer-AI interactions are predicated on bimodal behaviors: an acceleration mode, where AI is leveraged to quickly perform well-understood logic, and an exploration mode, where deep prompting, multi-suggestion comparisons, and line-by-line validation are used to resolve unfamiliar errors. Debugging AI-generated code requires active critical thinking rather than passive acceptance, especially when dealing with complex runtime errors or logic bugs in data pipelines.

### 3.3 Security Risks, Code Errors, and Overreliance

Despite the clear utility, recent literature highlights important cautionary notes regarding the risks of unrestrained GenAI usage. Comprehensive security analyses show that LLM-generated code can sometimes introduce subtle security vulnerabilities, syntax flaws, or hidden logic errors. Additionally, educational researchers warn of the risk of overreliance, especially among beginner programmers who may overestimate AI reliability. Simply taking AI output and using it without critical evaluation can lead to subtle plagiarism, erosion of basic problem-solving skills, and a reduction in the deep cognitive processing necessary to truly master software engineering principles.

## 4. Research Methodology

To investigate the role of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GenAI) in code generation and debugging among data engineering students, this study adopted a systematic literature review (SLR) approach. The methodology outlines the systematic process of gathering, screening, and evaluating relevant peer-reviewed literature to synthesize current insights.

### 4.1 Review Process and Approach

The review process consisted of three main stages:

- **Identification:** Formulating clear search strings and identifying appropriate digital repositories.
- **Screening:** Filtering retrieved papers based on predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria that focus on the title, abstract, and relevance to software or data engineering education.
- **Selection:** Reviewing full articles to ensure they directly address code generation, debugging behavior, productivity, or risks associated with AI assistants.

### 4.2 Digital Databases Used

- **Google Scholar:** For broad multidisciplinary coverage of open-access and preprint scholarly articles.
- **IEEE Xplore:** To capture peer-reviewed conference papers and journals focusing specifically on software engineering, computing education, and artificial intelligence.

### 4.3 Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

#### Inclusion Criteria

- Peer-reviewed journal articles, conference proceedings, and empirical studies.
- Literature published within the recent timeframe to reflect rapid advancements of modern GenAI models.
- Studies focusing on programming education, developer workflows, interaction modes, or software engineering learning outcomes.

#### Exclusion Criteria

- Non-academic sources such as blog posts, tutorials, and unverified news articles.
- Papers lacking full-text accessibility or written in languages other than English.
- Studies focusing purely on hardware engineering or non-programming domains without direct relevance to software or data engineering.
